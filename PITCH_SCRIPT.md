## Slide 1 — cover (0:20)

**On screen:** Title "homie." with the tagline "the people you know — known better."

**Intro / transition in:** "Hey everyone — quick one. Who I am, what we built this weekend."

**Bullets to hit:**
- Name yourself, name the team, one breath.
- Say the product name out loud: "homie."
- Say the tagline like you mean it: "the people you know, known better."
- Tease: "Quick story before I show you anything."

**Outro / transition out:** "So — let's start with a number that genuinely bothered me."

---

## Slide 2 — loneliness (0:35)

**On screen:** Two stats cards revealed on click — 27% of young Indians 19–29 are seriously lonely; 80% of Gen Z globally feels socially isolated.

**Intro / transition in:** "Two stats. One Indian, one global. Same story."

**Bullets to hit:**
- *(click 1)* "27% of young Indians, ages 19 to 29 — seriously lonely. Aspen Institute, last year."
- "That's basically one in four people in this room's age bracket."
- *(click 2)* "Globally — 80% of Gen Z says they feel socially isolated."
- "We're the most connected generation in history. And we've never been more alone."

**Outro / transition out:** "And it's not just our age group."

---

## Slide 3 — kids (0:35)

**On screen:** Two stats cards revealed on click — teens average 8h+ daily entertainment screen time; 40% of urban Indians felt MORE isolated despite being constantly online.

**Intro / transition in:** "It's worse for the kids and weirder for the rest of us."

**Bullets to hit:**
- *(click 1)* "Teenagers — 8 hours a day. Just on entertainment. Not school. Not homework. Eight hours of feed."
- "That's a full-time job, scrolling."
- *(click 2)* "And here's the kicker — 40% of urban Indians said they felt *more* isolated *because* they were always online."
- "More online. More alone. Something is broken."

**Outro / transition out:** "So I asked myself — why?"

---

## Slide 4 — problems (0:30)

**On screen:** Dense slide listing 5 problems with today's social apps, with FB, Insta, TikTok, X, Snap, YouTube icons scattered around.

**Intro / transition in:** "Look at this lineup. These are the apps in your pocket right now. Here's what they actually do."

**Bullets to hit:**
- "One — they're content machines. They're dopamine vending machines, not social networks."
- "Two — they don't grow your friendships. You scroll past your best friend to watch a stranger dance."
- "Three — they don't build real communities. Followers aren't a community."
- "Four — they don't respect your privacy. You are the product."
- "Five — they don't help you actually hang out. In real life. With humans."

**Outro / transition out:** "So we asked — what if a social app was actually social? Watch this."

---

## Slide 5 — graph (1:25 total)

**On screen:** A live, morphing node graph. 5 states, 4 clicks to advance. Each state visualises one problem getting solved.

**Intro / transition in:** "One graph. Five states. This is the whole product, visually."

### 5.1 — "no content" (~0:17)

**Bullets to hit:**
- "Start with you. Just you."
- *(click — feeds, reels, posts orbit in)* "This is what your apps look like today. Content, content, content."
- *(they fly into the bin)* "We threw it out. No feed. No algorithm. No reels. Gone."

### 5.2 — "real friendships" (~0:17)

**Bullets to hit:**
- *(click — friend nodes spawn)* "Now — the people you actually know. Friends, family, your colony."
- *(white interest nodes link them)* "And the things you share with each of them. Music. Books. The cafe you both love. *That's* a friendship."
- "homie remembers what connects you, so the app can actually help you act on it."

### 5.3 — "communities" (~0:17)

**Bullets to hit:**
- *(click — Venn regions appear: gaming, sports, music)* "People aren't just nodes. They're in groups."
- *(people glide in)* "Your gaming squad. The Sunday football guys. The playlist sharers. Real overlapping communities — not 'followers'."

### 5.4 — "privacy / business model" (~0:17)

**Bullets to hit:**
- *(click — businesses appear at the edge, mail flies to the community)* "Yes, businesses are here. But look where the message goes — to the *community*, not to you."
- "Your data never leaves your circle. Businesses talk to interest groups, not individuals. That's the whole privacy model in one picture."

### 5.5 — "IRL hangouts" (~0:17)

**Bullets to hit:**
- *(click — businesses fade, IRL zones appear: cafe, open mic, run club)* "And the endgame — you actually leave the app."
- "Coffee. Open mic. A run with the colony. The whole point of homie is to get you off homie."

**Outro / transition out:** "Okay — so what did we actually build? Three quick inventories."

## Slide 8 — child-protection (0:25)

## What to cut if you're behind on time

In order — cut from the top of this list first:

- **Slide 9 use-cases:** drop 9.2 (food) and 9.4 (run club). Saves ~24s. The other three carry the story.
- **Slide 6/7/8 inventories:** read only the first two bullets per slide instead of all four. Saves ~20s.
- **Slide 5 graph state 5.4 (privacy):** mention privacy in one line and click straight through. Saves ~10s.
- **Slide 3 kids:** drop the second stat and just gesture at it. Saves ~10s.
- **Last resort:** skip Slide 4 problems entirely — the graph (Slide 5) makes the same point visually. Saves ~30s.

Do **not** cut: cover, loneliness stats, the demo, the close.

---

## What to do if the demo video fails

1. **Don't panic. Don't apologise more than once.** "Demo gods aren't with us — let me walk you through it instead."
2. Open the live app on your laptop / phone — mirror to the screen if possible.
3. **The 45-second manual demo path:**
   - Open your friend graph — point at one friend, point at the shared interest node.
   - Open a community channel — show one post and one coupon.
   - Open the kid account screen — point at night-lock and contact gates.
4. If even the live app is broken, use the slides — flip back to Slide 5 graph state 5.2 and narrate the flow from there.
5. **Then keep moving.** Go straight to Slide 11. Don't try to recover lost time.
